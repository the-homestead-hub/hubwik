## What makes a grow lamp

<p>A lamp becomes a grow lamp when it is specified for plant work: a spectrum that overlaps photosynthetically active radiation, a photoperiod you can run, and a placement that actually reaches the canopy. A warm bulb in a living room is not that, even if a basil plant sits near it. A horticultural fixture with a published PPFD map is the other end of the scale.</p>
<p>Halo sits in the middle: a circular LED sold as kit-scale fill light for a Homestead Hub stack, mild warm-white, USB-powered. Seed treatises on this wick say six to eight hours of light. That is a growing instruction, not a laboratory rating of this ring.</p>

## Light, briefly

<p>Photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) is the waveband plants use for photosynthesis, conventionally 400–700 nanometres. Photosynthetic photon flux density (PPFD) is how many of those photons land on a square metre per second. Photoperiod is how long the lamp is on. McCree and later photobiology treat the waveband as a plant-physics fact, not a marketing colour name.</p>
<p>THH has not published a PPFD map, a spectrum plot, or a yield trial for Halo. This page will not invent one. If a study is added later, it will be named here with a link. Until then the honest sentence is: this is a kit-scale warm-white ring we distribute; the crop pages tell you to give the plant hours of light.</p>
